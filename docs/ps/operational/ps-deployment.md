## 1. Applicability

### Background
The CliniCall team is a provincial resource tasked with providing frontline clinical leadership, shared decision making, and risk mitigation support to protect BCEHS' people, patients, and infrastructure province-wide[cite: 10]. 

The CliniCall deployment model drives clinical excellence, safety leadership, and operational agility within BCEHS[cite: 10]. By integrating virtual clinical oversight through CliniCall with the dynamic use of Tango, the CliniCall team provides critical support where and when it is needed most, aligning deployment practices with high-acuity demand, CBRNE and HAZMAT safety, and collaborative field support[cite: 10].

### Purpose
This Decision Support Tool (DST) identifies the deployment framework for Paramedic Specialist resources, the circumstances necessitating a Paramedic Specialist response, and Paramedic Specialist dispatching[cite: 10].

### Scope
This procedure applies to paramedics when working as Clinical Hub Paramedic Specialists within Provincial Clinical Operations[cite: 10].

## 2. Procedure

### a. Paramedic Specialist Deployment Strategy
* Paramedic Specialists deploy as lone responders with the intent of providing advanced care and specialized technical coverage across the Metro Vancouver geographical area, with focused deployment in the Vancouver Coastal and northern Fraser health regions[cite: 10].
* As a Provincial resource, specific deployments outside the standard operating area (Metro Vancouver) may be identified by CliniCall and must be coordinated with the responsible Charge Emergency Medical Dispatcher (CEMD) and Provincial Operations Manager (POM)[cite: 10].
* As part of CliniCall outreach, Paramedic Specialists may request to be deployed to areas outside of Metro Vancouver to provide an ACP resource for the duration of the shift and promote CliniCall services (e.g., eastern Fraser Valley, Sea to Sky, and Sunshine Coast)[cite: 10]. These requests require advance approval from the Clinical Hub Manager (considering operational costs) and collaboration with local leadership of the desired area[cite: 10, 11].
* Paramedic Specialist deployment provides additional resources in a layered response for clinical leadership, technical advice, and crew support, irrespective of existing Advanced Care Paramedic (ACP) resources and MPDS coding[cite: 11].

### b. Paramedic Specialist Response Guidelines & Duties
* **Core Patient Care & Staff Support:** Both in dispatch and on the street, the primary focus is delivering advanced high-quality care, ensuring safety, and supporting staff in:
  * Events identified by the dispatch operations center as requiring advanced care paramedic response based on Clinical Response Model (CRM) priority (e.g., cardiac arrest, status seizure, multisystem trauma)[cite: 11].
  * Complex urgent and primary care events requiring advanced clinical assessment and shared decision-making[cite: 11].
  * Complex events requiring clinical leadership in collaboration with frontline leaders (On-Call Paramedic Supervisors - OCPS), such as multi-patient or mass casualty events[cite: 11].
* **CBRNE & HRH Safety Officers:** Paramedic Specialists are tasked as Safety Officers (per Incident Command System Structure) for chemical, biological, radiological, nuclear, and explosive (CBRNE) and High-Risk Hazard (HRH) events[cite: 11].
  * Assumes responsibility for safety on scene when dispatched in this capacity[cite: 11].
  * Holds final decisions on safety for staging, entry into scene, and decontamination of patients and equipment (refer to *BCEHS DST for Paramedic Specialist HAZMAT and HRH Review*)[cite: 11].
  * CliniCall is responsible for reviewing and recommending responses in this capacity[cite: 11].
  * A Tango (PS-PRU) unit may be diverted off an event prior to arrival at CliniCall's direction[cite: 11].
* **Layered Resource Support:** Paramedic Specialists may be layered to provide clinical support for high/low acuity calls or challenging events, including:
  * New ACPs attending High-Acuity-Low-Occurrence (HALO) events[cite: 11].
  * PRU and/or de-paired ACP members attending HALO events or performing high-risk procedures[cite: 12].
  * Any crew requesting a Paramedic Specialist or ACP resource, or any event requiring clinical support, leadership, or advanced care[cite: 12].
* Any request by CliniCall to have a Paramedic Specialist/Tango unit attached to a call must not be reasonably denied, with collaboration between CEMD and CliniCall to ensure appropriate resource assignment[cite: 13].
* **Crew Support, Education, & Strategic Goals:** Dispatched to provide:
  * Clinical/technical debriefings following HALO, HRH, and/or CBRNE events[cite: 13].
  * Support for BCEHS clinical trials, projects, and proofs of concept[cite: 13].
  * Continuing education campaigns in conjunction with BCEHS Learning and Paramedic Practice Educators[cite: 13].
* While conducting clinical pre-briefings and/or de-briefings, Paramedic Specialists must ensure the activation of the Critical Incident Stress (CIS) response team where appropriate[cite: 13].

### c. Paramedic Specialist Dynamic Deployment
* Paramedic Specialists must be deployed in PRU mode as the standard baseline[cite: 13].
* At the start of each shift and throughout, CliniCall (in collaboration with CEMD) must identify unstaffed advanced care paramedic resources to provide operational coverage in PRU mode[cite: 13].
* Once vacancies are filled, and after consulting with CliniCall and CEMD, Paramedic Specialists must dynamically deploy to enhance ACP resources where required, prioritizing channels lacking ACP-PRU resources[cite: 13]. Start locations may be modified by CEMD based on operational needs[cite: 13].
* Movement between channel boundaries within Metro Vancouver is normal practice as event volumes vary[cite: 13]. When crossing boundaries, Paramedic Specialists must contact appropriate channel operators, and if crossing Dispatch Operations Centre boundaries, coordinate with CEMD(s) and consider advising the POM[cite: 13].
* During large-scale events (provincially or in Metro Vancouver, e.g., Tumbler Ridge, Lapu Lapu), crews can respond from CliniCall to PRU or PRU to CliniCall via team collaboration to ensure proper resource deployment[cite: 13, 14].

### d. Paramedic Specialist Unit Status
* Paramedic Specialist resources must log in and depart Station 287 (Virtual Way) for their dynamic deployment area no later than **45 minutes** after shift commencement[cite: 20]. If unable due to equipment checks, orientation, or other necessities, they must go **Out of Service - Admin**[cite: 20].
* Units (callsign "Tango") deploy in **PRU** or **Tango (TNG)** status[cite: 20]:
  * **Default Status:** PRU mode[cite: 20].
  * **TNG Requests:** The first car leaving Virtual Way at 06:00H and 14:00H shift times may request TNG mode if they are a lone responder with no partner/student and no assigned ACP regional coverage[cite: 20].
  * **Manual Changes:** Remaining units can change to TNG mode during system pressures, mass gatherings, protests, or holidays, OR for dedicated responses to duties listed in section 2(b) 1–5[cite: 20].
  * **SAFETY NOTE:** In TNG mode, the PS resource does not present in Recommend Unit to ensure availability for TNG-specific events. Members must remain extra vigilant in scanning queues and informing dispatch of appropriate responses (e.g., nearby cardiac arrest)[cite: 20].
* Channel Operators must change deployment status from PRU to TNG when appropriate and in consultation with the PS resource and CliniCall[cite: 20].
* When mentoring or paired, the PS must stay in PRU mode unless specifically tasked with a TNG event[cite: 20].

### e. Paramedic Specialist Dispatch & the Clinical Response Model (CRM)
* **CRM TNG Triggers:** When in TNG mode, responses recommended by the CRM follow these criteria:
  * *CRM indicates 'TNG' or 'Consider TANGO':* Response indicated within a 45-minute geographical window (TNG Mode)[cite: 21]. Can be exceeded by CEMD or stood down at CliniCall's discretion; any decision not to send must be clearly documented by CliniCall[cite: 21].
  * *CRM indicates PURPLE (non-TNG):* PRU mode or TNG mode PS must be dispatched when they are the closest advanced care resource[cite: 21].
  * *CRM indicates RED (non-TNG):* PRU mode PS dispatched when closest advanced care resource. TNG mode PS dispatched in consultation with PS on-car and CliniCall based on operational need[cite: 21].
  * *CRM indicates ORANGE (non-TNG):* PRU mode PS dispatched per current PRU guidelines (concurrently with a PCP Code 3 crew, or Fire Department if unavailable)[cite: 21]. TNG mode PS will not be dispatched unless extenuating circumstances are identified with CliniCall[cite: 21].
* Additional calls may be identified by CliniCall, Dispatch Supervisor, CEMD, Dispatcher, or crew irrespective of CRM/MPDS, and assigned when appropriate via discussion between CliniCall and Charge EMD[cite: 21].
* **SAFETY NOTE:** Aligning with the Lone Responder safety plan, the Paramedic Specialist must be dispatched concurrently with a PCP crew Code 3, or Fire Department if unavailable[cite: 21].
* Where conflicts arise with CRM, CliniCall coordinates with CEMD on a call-by-call basis for PS deployment (including PS-initiated events), ensuring clear documentation and coordination with channel operators[cite: 22]. Paramedic Specialists may also request assignment to calls via channel operators[cite: 22].

### f. Shift Times
* Tango resources must remain in service until the completion of their end-of-shift time[cite: 22].
* "Out of Service-Admin" requires a reasonable explanation and request through the channel operator or OCPS[cite: 22].
* Members may return to Station 287 (Virtual Way) 30 minutes prior to shift end but must stay available via portable radio[cite: 22].
* Meal breaks and restocking under the dynamic deployment model should be completed at the nearest ACP station unless extenuating circumstances arise (e.g., personal items, soiled uniform)[cite: 22].
